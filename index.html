    Reading room

    Understand the numbers

    Nominal return

    How much the number of dollars grows. $100 that becomes $110 is a 10% nominal return.

    Inflation

    How much prices rise. At 3% inflation, a $100 basket costs $103 a year later. Each dollar buys less.

    Real return

    How much more you can buy. 10% growth with 3% inflation is (1.10 / 1.03) − 1 = 6.8% real.

    Purchasing power

    What one dollar from your start year buys at the end. Since 2005 it fell to about 59 cents.

    Questions

    Is a 7% real return from the S&P 500 realistic?

    From 1928 to 2025 the S&P 500 returned 6.8% per year after inflation (10.0% before inflation), with dividends reinvested. The path is not smooth. 20-year real returns went from 0.6% per year (1929–1948) to 13.2% per year (1980–1999). The "Does holding longer help?" section shows every period.

    Has the S&P 500 ever lost money after inflation over 20 years?

    Not in calendar-year data from 1928 to 2025. Every 20-year period gained purchasing power. Over 10 years it is different: 11 of 89 periods lost money after inflation. The worst was 1999–2008, at −3.8% per year.

    Why do you use CPI and not another inflation measure?

    CPI-U is the most common measure of US consumer prices and has data back to 1913. The Fed targets PCE inflation, which is usually about 0.3 points lower per year. With PCE, real returns would be a little higher. Your own inflation rate depends on what you buy.

    Why December to December?

    Returns are for calendar years (January 1 to December 31). December-to-December CPI measures inflation over the same 12 months. An annual-average CPI would be about six months out of step with the returns.

    Do these numbers include dividends, fees and taxes?

    They include dividends, reinvested. They do not include fund fees or taxes. Set them with the sliders in the calculator. Even 1% per year in costs takes a large share of a 7% real return over 30 years.

    Why do my numbers differ from other sites?

    Sources calculate total return in slightly different ways (dividend timing, index version). This site uses one source for all years: Aswath Damodaran's NYU Stern data. For example, it shows 2025 at +17.7%, and some sites show +17.9%. Over many years the difference is small.

    I do not live in the US. Does this apply to me?

    Not directly. If you buy the S&P 500 in another currency, your return also includes the exchange rate, and your own country's inflation is what matters. A US investor and a euro investor can get quite different real returns from the same index.

    What is the S&P 500?

    An index of about 500 large US companies, weighted by market value. It covers about 80% of the value of the US stock market. .

    Sources & method

    Annual total returns: Aswath Damodaran, NYU Stern (S&P 500 with dividends, 3-month T-bill, 10-year Treasury, Baa corporate bond, US real estate, gold). Inflation: CPI-U, not seasonally adjusted, December over December (BLS, through FRED). Monthly deposits are added at the end of each month, with the year's return and inflation spread evenly across its months. Fees and tax drag reduce the return each year.
